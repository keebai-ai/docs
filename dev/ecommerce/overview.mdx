The ecommerce surface exposes what Keebai knows about your **catalog**, the **carts** your customers built (including the ones they abandoned), and the **sales** that closed. On top of that, it lets your own point of sale or ERP manage **stock**, **record sales**, and issue their **electronic tax documents** (DTE) — everything under the `/v1/ecommerce` prefix, authenticated with a [PAT](/dev/api-keys) and gated by [scope](/dev/scopes).

Products and carts stay read-only: creating or editing a product goes through the portal or the platform integration, not this API.

A cart is **not** the draft of a sale. They are separate records with no foreign key between them: a cart is what a customer assembled on a storefront, a sale is what your team or the agent closed. Correlate on the customer's phone or email, or on `customer_id`, which both carry when Keebai could resolve the contact.

## Scopes

| Scope | Grants |
|---|---|
| `ecommerce:products:read` | [List](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-products-list) and [read](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-product-get) the catalog. |
| `ecommerce:carts:read` | [List](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-carts-list) and [read](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-cart-get) carts, including abandoned ones. |
| `ecommerce:sales:read` | [List](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-sales-list) and [read](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-sale-get) sales. |
| `ecommerce:sales:write` | [Create sales](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-sales-create) and internal consumptions. |
| `ecommerce:stock:read` | [Read stock](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-product-stock) and [stock movements](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-stock-movements). |
| `ecommerce:stock:write` | [Adjust](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-stock-adjust) and [transfer](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-stock-transfer) stock. |
| `ecommerce:invoicing:read` | [Read the issuable DTE types](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-invoicing-types), [a sale's documents](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-sale-documents), and [their PDF](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-invoicing-document-pdf). |
| `ecommerce:invoicing:issue` | [Issue a sale's DTE](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-sale-dte-issue). |

The read scopes are separate because they leak different things. Carts and sales carry customer contact details and money; the catalog carries neither. A pricing widget on your website needs `ecommerce:products:read` and nothing else.

<Warning>
  **The scopes carry the role's permissions.** On every call the API passes the ecommerce service the permissions that match the token's scopes, and those scopes were already limited to the user's role when the token was minted. Minting a token with `ecommerce:stock:write` or `ecommerce:invoicing:issue` requires those permissions in your role — adjusting and transferring stock, or issuing DTE — unless you are the company owner or a superuser.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  **Reads are company-wide.** Like [CRM](/dev/crm/overview#tenancy-is-implicit), these endpoints return records from every project in your company, not just the project your token belongs to.
</Warning>

## Products come from two places

| `type` | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Internal | Created in the Keebai portal. `external_id` is absent. |
| Integration | Synced from Shopify, WooCommerce, Justo, and the like. `external_id` and `integration_id` are set, and edits made in Keebai are overwritten on the next sync. |

`stock` is only meaningful when `stock_managed` is `true`. On integration products, stock is whatever the last sync brought over — not a live read of the platform.

## Inventory

A variant's stock has one **master total**. Part of it is **allocated** to branches and the rest is **available in the warehouse**. [`GET /v1/ecommerce/products/{id}/stock`](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-product-stock) returns all three, plus the quantity per branch.

| Operation | Effect on the total | Endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| Adjust | Changes it: a positive `delta` is an entry, a negative one an exit. | [`POST .../stock/adjust`](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-stock-adjust) |
| Transfer | None. Units only move between the warehouse and branches. | [`POST .../stock/transfer`](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-stock-transfer) |
| Sale | Deducts from the sale's branch. | [`POST /v1/ecommerce/sales`](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-sales-create) |

Every change leaves an entry in [the stock movements](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-stock-movements), and adjustments are synced to the stores you have connected. To find the `product_id` and `variant_id` from a scanned code, use [`GET /v1/ecommerce/products/find`](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-products-find).

## Recording sales and invoicing

[`POST /v1/ecommerce/sales`](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-sales-create) records a sale from your own point of sale: it deducts stock, calculates commissions, and, with `dte`, issues the boleta or factura right after. A `type: "internal"` sale records internal consumption instead, with no payments and no DTE.

Send an `Idempotency-Key` header so a retry after a timeout does not create the sale twice.

A DTE that fails does not undo the sale. It comes back in `dte.failed` with its reason, and you issue it later with [`POST /v1/ecommerce/sales/{id}/dte`](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-sale-dte-issue). Check which types the company can issue today with [`GET /v1/ecommerce/invoicing/types`](/dev/endpoints/ecommerce-invoicing-types).

## Abandoned carts

A cart with `abandoned_at` set and `completed_at` absent is the recoverable case. `recovery_status` tracks what has been done about it:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pending` | Nobody has followed up. |
| `contacted` | Someone reached out — a person or the agent. |
| `recovered` | The customer came back and completed. |
| `discarded` | Written off. |

`recovery_url` is the link that puts the customer back in checkout with the cart intact. Sending it over WhatsApp with [a `cta_url` message](/dev/endpoints/messages-send) is the intended pairing.

<Warning>
  **`recovery_status` is not advanced by this API.** It is set by whoever does the following up — the portal, the agent, or an integration writing through the platform. Reading `pending` does not mean nobody has acted; it means nothing has recorded that they did.

  If your own outreach chases abandoned carts, you are responsible for keeping your side's state, because you cannot write this field back.
</Warning>

A cart becomes abandoned on a timer, not on an explicit action, so `abandoned_at` trails the customer's last activity. Do not treat a cart as fresh just because `abandoned_at` is recent.

## Identifiers

All ids are MongoDB ObjectIds — 24 lowercase hex characters. That covers products, carts, sales, and the `customer_id` that carts and sales carry when Keebai could resolve the buyer to a [contact](/dev/endpoints/contacts-list).

`external_id` is different: it is the id the record has **in the source platform** (Shopify, WooCommerce, Justo), in that platform's own format. Use it to join back to your storefront; never pass it where Keebai expects an `ObjectId`.

## Tenancy is implicit

Company comes from your token and is never sent. Sending `company` returns `400`.

As the warning above notes, reads span the whole company rather than one project — a token minted for project A lists products, carts, and sales created under project B.

## Correlating with the rest of the platform

Ecommerce records are deliberately loosely coupled, which means joining them is your job:

| From | To | Join on |
|---|---|---|
| Cart or sale | A messaging contact | `customer_id`, or fall back to phone / email |
| Cart | Sale | Nothing direct — match the customer plus a time window |
| Sale | A [CRM ticket](/dev/crm/overview) | Nothing direct — the `ecommerce.order_created` [CRM event](/dev/crm/events) lands on the contact's ticket, which is the supported path |
| Product | Your storefront | `external_id` + `integration_id` |

If you need a hard link between a sale and a CRM ticket, [emit a CRM event](/dev/endpoints/crm-events-emit) carrying your order id in a ticket-scoped custom field. That writes the id onto the ticket, which is the closest thing to a foreign key the platform offers.

## Money is stored in minor units

Every amount — `price`, `unit_price`, `subtotal`, `total`, and the whole `amounts` block on a sale — is an integer in the currency's smallest unit. Read `currency` alongside it, and never assume two decimals: `JPY` and `CLP` have none.

## Pagination

List endpoints take `limit` and `offset` and return the same envelope as the rest of the API:

```json
{ "data": [], "total": 0, "limit": 50, "offset": 0 }
```

`limit` defaults to 50 and caps at 200.

<Note>
  The catalog paginates by page internally, so an `offset` that is not a multiple of `limit` is rounded down to the page containing it. Keep `offset` a multiple of `limit` — the standard `offset += limit` loop is always correct.
</Note>

## Errors

| Status | When |
|---|---|
| `400` | An invalid query parameter or body, or one the endpoint does not accept. |
| `401` | Missing, invalid, revoked, or expired token. |
| `403` | The token lacks the scope the endpoint requires. |
| `404` | No record with that id, or it belongs to a different company. |
| `422` | A business rule rejected a sale, such as a missing branch, an invalid payment, or not enough stock. |
| `429` | You hit a [rate limit](/dev/rate-limits). |
| `502` | `ECOMMERCE_UPSTREAM_ERROR`: the ecommerce service could not answer. |

Branch on `error.code`, not on the message text. A `4xx` keeps the business code the ecommerce service returned, such as `STOCK_INSUFFICIENT_IN_BRANCH` or `SKU_NOT_FOUND`.

## Rate limits

These endpoints draw from the shared [per-project quota](/dev/rate-limits): 1,000 requests a day across the whole public API.

That is the binding constraint for anyone mirroring a catalog. A 4,000-product catalog at `limit=200` is 20 requests — fine. The same catalog re-synced every 15 minutes is 1,920 a day — not fine.
